Now watching: Exodus (1960)

The method

Five questions for every film

The questions follow Jens Beckert: a promised future is legitimate when it is desirable and can plausibly be reached. Each question has a yes, partly or no answer, so film by film the answers add up to a pattern. Each answer is backed by a scene, with its timecode. Every film is also given one of four future types: integrative, restoration, retrotopian or anomic.

01

Promise

Does the film show a future in which the conflict is over?

02

Whose future

Is there a place in that future for the other side?

03

Belief

Do the characters believe the promise can be kept?

04

Cost

Is the price of the promise shown, and who pays it?

05

Past

When the future grows uncertain, does the film draw its legitimacy from the Holocaust?

The question behind the log

From promise to memory?

Jens Beckert argues that a social order stays legitimate while it can promise a credible future, and that this promissory legitimacy erodes when promises fail. The hypothesis tested here: as the screen stops promising a future, it leans more on the past. If so, the two lanes below should part: promise falling away while the past stays.

Every film also gets a file

Who funded, approved or shaped the production. Each file carries one level of evidence; the same rules apply to every state and company.

Institutional

A state body is documented as funding, approving, supplying or banning the work.

Personal tie

A creator has a documented link to an army, agency or government, by their own account or public record.

Portrayal only

The work depicts a state or agency; no involvement in the production is claimed.

Not published

Claims without a document or a first-hand account stay in research notes.
