Transformers2007, dir. Bay, number 5 worldwide that year

machine alliance

The political imagination in which the camera record — a Gulf base that needs no explanation, the deployed soldier as "us", a secret agency that is secretly good, machines as allies — coincides with the ground record: CENTCOM's forward base in Qatar, the surge's peak, warrantless surveillance, and a USD 160 billion bet on robotic war. The present order is restored, with better machines.

Record 009. Filed 21.10.2026. Ending coded restoration, zero-sum.

The film shows what the film puts forward. The record shows what was enacted, on record, at the same time.

Construction

What is built as problem, what is left as given

The filmThe film opens on a US special-operations base in Qatar destroyed by an alien machine. The attackers are not human, so the war needs no cause. The American presence in the Gulf is given: the base is simply there.
The recordThe presence was on record. Since 2003 Qatar's Al Udeid Air Base has hosted the forward headquarters of US Central Command, with flights to Afghanistan and Iraq; by 2017 about 10,000 US troops were stationed there.
Sources and selection note
  1. Al Jazeera (2017). US military: no plans to change our posture in Qatar. 6 June 2017.

Selection note. The troop figure dates from 2017; a figure for 2007 would satisfy the same-period condition better.

Address

Who stands in for the species

The filmTwo "we"s carry the film: a teenage boy in an American suburb, and a squad of US soldiers who survive the desert attack and fight the machines at home.
The recordThe soldiers stood for a force at its largest. As of 1 November 2007, during the surge, 164,424 US troops were in Iraq, nearly 20,000 of them from the National Guard and Reserves.
Sources and selection note
  1. O'Bryant, J. & Waterhouse, M. (2007). U.S. Forces in Iraq. Congressional Research Service, RS22449, updated 30 November 2007.

Selection note. Considered: Iraqi civilian deaths in 2007. Not filed here: the attention asks who stands in for "us", and the film's "us" is the deployed soldier.

Exclusion

Which carriers of the same keyword stay off-record

The filmA secret agency, Sector Seven, has hidden alien technology for decades, answers to no one, and turns out to be on the right side. Secrecy is played for laughs and then for victory.
The recordThe same word carries another file. On 17 December 2005 the President acknowledged that he had authorised the National Security Agency to intercept communications after 2001 without court approval, and said he had reauthorised the programme more than 30 times.
Sources and selection note
  1. CNN (2005). Bush says he signed NSA wiretap order. 17 December 2005.

Selection note. Exclusion may range historically. Considered: CIA secret detention, acknowledged in September 2006. Not filed: domestic surveillance is closer to Sector Seven's watching of Americans.

Prescription

What the framing proposes as future-reality

The filmThe remedy is an alliance of soldiers and machines. Good robots fight beside US troops, F-22s and an Osprey; at the end the Autobots' leader invites more machines to come to Earth.
The recordThe same prescription was the Army's plan. In March 2007 the Government Accountability Office reviewed the Future Combat System, a network of manned and unmanned ground and air vehicles costed by the Army at about USD 160 billion and independently at up to USD 234 billion, and found most of its technologies immature.
Sources and selection note
  1. US Government Accountability Office (2007). Defense Acquisitions: Key Decisions to Be Made on Future Combat System. GAO-07-376, 15 March 2007.

Selection note. Considered: F-22 procurement. Not filed: the Future Combat System matches the film's teaming of soldiers and machines.

Circulation

The infrastructure that carried the film

The film
Production supportAbout 300 airmen and soldiers on screen; filming at Edwards and Holloman Air Force Bases and White Sands Missile Range; F-117, CV-22 Osprey and F-22 Raptor; producers reimbursed flight costs.
Script involvementReviewed under the Pentagon's entertainment liaison office (Phil Strub); Capt. Christian Hodge served as the Defense Department project officer.
DistributionParamount and DreamWorks worldwide release; USD 710M.
AuthorityOfficial Air Force news stories promoted the production and its airmen.
The record
On the same channelsUS security agencies worked on over 800 films and 1,000 television titles, according to records obtained under freedom-of-information requests.
Off the same channelsThe surveillance programme reached the public through a newspaper leak, which the President called illegal, not through the channels that carried the film.
Sources
  1. Miles, D. (n.d.). Movie makers team with military to create realism. American Forces Press Service.
  2. University of Bath (2017). US government influence on films and television. 28 June 2017.
