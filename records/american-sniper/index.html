IConstruction

What is built as problem, what is left as given

Camera recordA threat is present in the Iraqi city street. A figure approaches — a woman, a child. The soldier must judge in seconds. The film constructs the problem as moral discrimination under pressure: who is a combatant, who is not, all decided through the rifle scope. Iraq itself is given as a hostile environment, etiology unspecified.
Ground recordThe 2003 invasion of Iraq, framed by the camera as setting, is the unnamed cause of the threat the soldier discriminates within. Iraq Body Count documents 150,772 civilian deaths from violence between 2003 and the end of 2014. The film's given is the ground record's consequence.

Sources

  1. Iraq Body Count (2015). Iraq 2014: Civilian deaths almost doubling year on year. 1 January 2015.

Selection note

Considered: the Lancet mortality surveys (2004, 2006). Not filed: IBC's documented count is the more conservative and better-sourced figure.

IIAddress

Who stands in for the species

Camera recordChris Kyle, his family, his unit, his town. The camera holds American faces: the wife on the phone, the brother in country, the child at home. The Iraqi bodies in the frame are framed through the scope — at distance, at the moment of judgement, mostly without name.
Ground recordIn 2014 alone, the year the film was released, IOM identified 2.1 million Iraqis internally displaced. They never address the camera as "we." The film's "we" is national; the ground record's "we" is unavailable to it.

Sources

  1. IOM Displacement Tracking Matrix (2014). Iraq IDP crisis, Round XI report, 25 December 2014.

Selection note

Considered: UNHCR's figure for Iraqi refugees abroad. Not filed: internal displacement in the release year is closer to the film's Iraqi setting.

IIIExclusion

Which carriers of the same keyword stay off-record

Camera recordThe word protection structures the film. Kyle protects his unit, his family, the children he will not let be killed by their parents. Protection is the moral framework of his vocation, the answer to why he returns.
Ground recordThe same word carries another file. Protection in the doctrinal vocabulary of the period — the responsibility to protect, force protection, civilian protection — recurs as the language under which interventions were authorised; ground records of those interventions show protection as a condition the protected often did not consent to or survive.

Sources

  1. United Nations General Assembly (2005). 2005 World Summit Outcome. A/RES/60/1, paragraphs 138–139.
  2. Costs of War Project (n.d.). Watson Institute for International and Public Affairs, Brown University.

Selection note

Exclusion may range historically; the 2005 doctrine is filed because it fixes the term's institutional meaning in the period.

IVPrescription

What the framing proposes as future-reality

Camera recordThe remedy is the soldier. Not policy reform, not withdrawal, not the renegotiation of the war. Kyle returns to country four times; the closing dedicates the film to him. The soldier's vocation is positioned as the moral form available to the audience.
Ground recordThe same prescription operates at policy level: the framing of military service as the locus of national moral life, the soldier as moral subject of foreign action, the absorption of policy questions into questions of individual valor and sacrifice.

Sources

  1. Bacevich, A. J. (2013). Breach of Trust: How Americans Failed Their Soldiers and Their Country. New York: Metropolitan Books.
  2. Lutz, C. (Ed.) (2009). The Bases of Empire: The Global Struggle against U.S. Military Posts. New York: NYU Press. [Author to confirm this is the intended Lutz 2009.]

Selection note

Interpretive pairing, resting on scholarship rather than a policy document.

VCirculation

The infrastructure that pre-clears the framing

Camera record
Production supportNo formal Department of Defense production assistance found in the sources checked. Former Navy SEAL Kevin Lacz served as technical adviser.
Script involvementBased on Chris Kyle's memoir (2012, with Scott McEwen and Jim DeFelice), which the Department of Defense vetted and partly redacted before publication.
Distribution channelsWarner Bros. global release; Village Roadshow co-production; largest January opening on record at the time (USD 89.2M, January 2015).
Authority infrastructureVeteran community endorsement; Academy nominations; box-office performance positioned the film as cultural settlement on the Iraq war.
Ground record
On the same channelsUS security agencies worked on over 800 films and 1,000 television titles, according to entertainment-liaison records obtained under freedom-of-information requests.
Off the same channelsIraqi cinema's record of the same war does not move through the same studio and distribution architecture; the Iraqi camera has no equivalent pre-clearance.

saviour soldier

The political imagination in which the camera record — the soldier as moral subject, protection as vocation, the rifle scope as instrument of ethical judgement — coincides with the ground record: the policy framework in which military service absorbs the political question of war and individual valor stands in for collective accountability. Film and policy travel as two registers of one imagination; the camera grants the soldier the moral authority that the ground record withholds from the policy.
