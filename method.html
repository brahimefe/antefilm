Method

How the films are read

Zionism, like every national movement, tells a story about the future: who it is for, what it costs and why it is deserved. This site reads that story in influential films and series, one at a time, with the same questions. It studies a political ideology on screen; it does not speak about Jews or Judaism as such.

Five questions

Asked of every film

A promised future holds while it seems both desirable and within reach. The questions follow that idea, from the promise itself to what props it up. Each is answered yes, partly or no, and each answer is tied to a scene.

01

Promise

Does the film show a future in which the conflict is over?

02

Whose future

Is there a place in that future for Palestinians?

03

Belief

Do the characters believe the promise can be kept?

04

Cost

Is the price of the promise shown, and who pays it?

05

Past

Does the film ground its future in the Holocaust?The question is about how memory is used in the film, never about the memory itself.

Every film is also given one of four future types: integrative, restoration, retrotopian or anomic. The questions will be revised as the files accumulate.

Eight periods

A working periodisation

The first four periods follow Ella Shohat's history of Israeli cinema; they are genres as much as eras and overlap in time. The last four extend it past the 1980s and take in Hollywood and streaming productions.

Heroic-nationalist1950s–1970s · Shohat

Founding struggle, pioneer heroes, the state as the end of the story.

Bourekas1960s–1970s · Shohat

Ethnic comedies of Ashkenazi and Mizrahi tension: whose future inside the nation.

Personal cinema1960s–1970s · Shohat

Auteur films that step back from the national story to the individual.

The Palestinian wave1980s · Shohat

Israeli films that put the Arab–Israeli encounter at the centre.

Oslo and the promise tested1990s · added

The peace process as a promise: does the screen take it up?

Shooting and crying2000s · added

After the second intifada: conscience, trauma and the soldier's guilt.

Security export and the platforms2010s–2023 · added

Israeli formats and intelligence stories travel to global streaming.

After 7 October2023– · added

An open period, followed as it happens.

Before the film

Evidence rules

Each film also gets a file on who funded, helped or sold it. Every item carries one level, and the same rules apply to every state and company.

Institutional

A state body is documented as funding, approving, supplying or banning the work.

Personal tie

A creator has a documented link to an army, agency or government, by their own account or public record.

Portrayal only

The work depicts a state or agency; no involvement in the production is claimed.

Not published as fact

Claims without a document or a first-hand account. Where a claim is widely repeated, the file records the dispute, not the claim.
