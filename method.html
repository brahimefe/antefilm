What antefilm records, and how.

The method is open for revision. Each change is dated in the data changelog.

1. What qualifies

Antefilm records global cinema only: films that reached the world at scale. The qualifying threshold is the annual worldwide top ten by box-office gross, from 2001 to the present. The reasoning is structural, not statistical: a film that did not travel at global scale did not shape the political imagination it is being asked to bear. Festival-scale films and minor releases belong to other readings.

Every qualifying film is coded in the atlas. Selected films also receive a full record, a close reading under the protocol below.

2. The film as one register of an imagined future

Antefilm follows Comyn (2018) in reading mass form as part of how a political imagination operates, not as its reflection. It follows Beckert (2016) in treating that imagination as an imagined future: a fictional expectation, an as-if picture of what is to come that orients action under uncertainty. A film at global scale is one register in which such expectations are staged and made available. It is not their cause.

The protocol therefore does not claim that a film produces a future. It records which future a film makes available, and whether that future can still carry promissory legitimacy: the belief that an envisioned future is desirable and can plausibly be reached by the means proposed (Beckert 2026).

Beckert (2026) diagnoses a condition of futural anomie. The capacities that once sustained integrative visions of collective progress have eroded, and the futures that remain are fragmented: collapse, technological salvation and escape, or return to an idealised past. He calls futural anomie both a symptom and a mirror of present conditions. Antefilm records the mirror.

Beckert notes that contemporary future-oriented fiction is overwhelmingly dystopian, but leaves that observation outside his analysis. Antefilm treats it as a claim to be tested across the corpus, not assumed. Beckert also limits his diagnosis to the Global North; because global top-ten films circulate worldwide, the address attention records whose future each film imagines.

3. The infrastructure that pre-clears the framing

A film at mass scale is never the work of its director alone. Production support, script involvement, distribution channels and authority infrastructure together pre-clear the framing as future-reality. Robb (2004) provided the first FOIA-based archive of Hollywood–Pentagon coordination; Alford and Secker (2017) extended it through entertainment-liaison records. Antefilm takes these four categories as standing rubrics under the circulation attention, applied to every record regardless of which state agency, scientific institution or studio is involved.

4. The five attentions

Each record holds five attentions, applied in the same order to every film. They are not stages of reading; they are dimensions of one record.

  1. Construction: what is built as problem, what is left as given.
  2. Address: who stands in for the species.
  3. Exclusion: which carriers of the same keyword stay off-record.
  4. Prescription: what the framing proposes as future-reality.
  5. Circulation: the infrastructure that pre-clears the framing.

5. Camera record and ground record

For each attention, the record holds two columns. The left, the camera record, holds what the film puts forward: what it shows, says, hails. The right, the ground record, holds what is already in operation, on record, under the same political imagination. The two are not text and context. They are two operations of one configuration: one rendered through the camera, one rendered through institutional and operational documentation. On each record page they are set in two typefaces, so the two registers stay visibly distinct.

For some films, especially the few with integrative endings, the ground record runs against the camera record rather than with it: the future the film imagines is not the one being enacted. These are kept as divergence records and marked as such in their selection notes. Divergence is itself a finding: it shows an imagination circulating on screen without being built on the ground.

6. How a ground record is chosen

A ground record is filed beside a camera record only if it meets four conditions.

  1. Same term or construction. It is on record under the term the camera record carries (departure, rescue, protection), or under the same construction of the problem.
  2. Same agent. It is enacted by an institution that appears in the camera record or in the film's circulation: the state, agency or industry the film depicts or that cooperated with it.
  3. Same period. For construction, address and prescription, it falls within the film's period band. Exclusion may range historically, because it records the term's other file.
  4. Institutional source. It rests on an institutional or scholarly record: a UN agency, government document, FOIA release, court record or peer-reviewed dataset. Commentary alone does not qualify.

Each attention also carries a short selection note, shown in the margin, naming the strongest alternative ground record considered and why it was not filed. Where a record falls short of a condition, its selection note says so. The pairing records coincidence, not causation.

7. Naming the imagination

Each record carries a named imagination: a short formulation of the political imagination the camera and ground records jointly carry. The name is not interpretation; it is a working file label. Exit imperialism is the imagination filed for Interstellar. As the archive grows, the named imaginations form an atlas of which configurations recur, which periods carry which grammars, and which films coincide in their political work.

8. Coding the corpus

Every film in the corpus, filed or not, is coded on two variables that make Beckert's claim testable. Both code the camera record: the future as the film resolves it, not antefilm's reading of it.

Imaginary type: integrative (the future as collective progress open to all); anomic: collapse (breakdown to be endured, or destruction as the path to a purified order); anomic: escape (transformation delegated to technology, escape or survival for a select group); retrotopian (return to an idealised past order, after Bauman 2017); restoration (no new future: the present order is defended or restored); personal (no collective future is at stake).

Payoff structure: positive-sum (gains reach everyone, including outsiders to the protagonists' group); zero-sum (insiders are secured through the defeat or exclusion of an out-group, a people, species or nation, not merely an individual villain); negative-sum (general loss; at most a few survive).

Restoration and personal are antefilm's additions to Beckert's types; most franchise films fall under them. The present coding is provisional: it was drafted with the assistance of an AI model and is under the editor's review. Films the coder could not place with confidence are marked as not yet coded rather than guessed. The codebook and data are on the data page.

9. How a record is added

A new record opens when (a) the film meets the qualifying threshold, (b) sources can be filed beside the camera record on each attention under the rule in §6, and (c) a named imagination can be formulated. Records still under the editor's review are marked as drafts.

10. Limits and reflexivity

The protocol records two registers: the camera record and the ground record. Neither register carries the voice of those it names. The bodies enumerated in the ground record — displaced, harmed, counted by UNHCR, by FAO, by FOIA — are not spoken for by antefilm. The ground record is institutional record: the way the same political imagination is enacted at the level of state, military and humanitarian apparatuses. Antefilm registers the coincidence of two such institutional registers; it does not claim to register the lives the institutions count.

A different protocol, organised around oral, vernacular or community records, would be a different project, and one this protocol does not preclude. The present record acknowledges its limit at the point where the institutional register ends and another register would have to begin.

11. Data model

Each record conforms to a formal data model, published as schema.json. It fixes the field structure of the five attentions, the camera, ground, sources and selection-note subfields, the four Robb-derived rubrics under circulation, and the two corpus variables. The model allows the corpus to be queried, exported or extended without dependence on the present site.
